How this simulator works

      
Left: circuit and code. The ESP32-WROOM-32 DevKit V1 (30 pins) sits in its GVS expansion board. Every GPIO has its own S (signal), V and G (GND) pin; the jumper sets V to 3.3 V or 5 V. Drag a part from Components onto the workplane, click a pin to start a wire, click empty space to bend it, then click another pin to finish. Select a part to see its connections, warnings and sensor controls. Open Code to edit the sketch, then press Start Simulation.

      
Right: IoT Cloud. In Things → Setup, ADD a cloud variable: thingProperties.h and the onXxxChange() callback are written for you. On Dashboards, click the pencil, ADD a widget and link it to a variable. A switch or slider reaches the board on the next ArduinoCloud.update(). Devices has the simulated Wi-Fi router; Triggers sends simulated email and push alerts.

      
What is simulated

      
    
        
  • GPIO, INPUT_PULLUP/PULLDOWN, interrupts, 12-bit ADC (0–4095), PWM (analogWrite, ledcWrite), tone(), DAC
    • 
        
  • Wiring: power and GND checks, LED polarity and series resistor, 3.3 V vs 5 V supply, shorts, input-only pins 34–39, ADC2 pins with WiFi on, strapping pins
    • 
        
  • Wi-Fi join and retry, cloud connection, property sync (on change, periodic, threshold), cloud-wins sync on connect
    • 
        
  • Libraries: DHT, ESP32Servo, Wire, LiquidCrystal_I2C, WiFi, ArduinoIoTCloud, Serial
    • 
      

      
Limits

      
    
        
  • The sketch is translated to JavaScript in your browser, not compiled for the Xtensa CPU. Timing is approximate.
    • 
        
  • Plain sketch C++ only: no class/struct, templates, or pointers beyond strings and arrays.
    • 
        
  • Nothing leaves your browser. There is no real Wi-Fi or MQTT traffic, and alerts are not really sent.
